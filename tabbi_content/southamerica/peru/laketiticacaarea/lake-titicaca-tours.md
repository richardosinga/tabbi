Lake Titicaca is the highest navigable lake in the world and one of South America's most distinctive travel experiences. The main tour circuit from Puno covers the Uros floating islands — pre-Inca communities living on artificial reed platforms, now partially oriented to tourism but still genuinely inhabited — and Taquile Island, a natural island of Inca terracing with a weaving tradition recognised by UNESCO. Amantaní Island, less visited, offers overnight homestays with island families. Day tours cover Uros and Taquile; two-day packages add Amantaní. The lake sits at 3,812 metres, which means cold temperatures year-round and a strong UV exposure — warm layers and sun protection are essential regardless of the weather.
